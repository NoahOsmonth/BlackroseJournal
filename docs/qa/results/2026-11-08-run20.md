# QA run 20 — prompts that follow you (#52)

Follow-up to #13. The shelf now offers one prompt built from what the writer kept returning to this week.

## What the writer sees

Today, after three entries mentioning sleep:

> **This morning · In my body · Sleep**  ← was "Something good"

Tapping **Sleep** opens the chat with:

> More about sleep and how it has been lately: ▍

In their own voice, ready to finish.

## The rules it follows

- The theme comes from `collectWeeklyThemes` (#10), so it is **a word the writer wrote**, never a label we invented.
- Only the **last slot** is replaced, so at least one generic prompt always survives.
- An empty week gives back **exactly** today's shelf — no personalisation, no blank shelf.
- The same theme is never offered twice.

## Live verified

1. Baseline shelf: `This morning · In my body · Something good`.
2. Wrote three entries across three days, all mentioning sleep.
3. Reloaded: shelf became `This morning · In my body · **Sleep**`.
4. The chip's accessibility label read `Start with: More about sleep and how it has been lately:`.
5. Removed the three probe entries and reloaded: shelf returned to `This morning · In my body · Something good`.

Chip measured 44 px tall with the `hairline-dark` border (`rgb(44, 42, 38)`) — the real token, not a black fallback.

## Why it is safe to trust

The theme is exactly the one already shown in the "What kept coming back" card. If that card is empty, the shelf cannot personalise — the two surfaces share one detector, so they cannot disagree.

## A measuring mistake worth recording

My first two attempts to seed test entries by hand wrote into localStorage in the wrong shape and silently did nothing — the journal shards store entries as an **object keyed by id**, not an array. Two rounds were spent concluding "the feature does not work" when the *test fixture* was wrong. The fix was to read the real shape first, then write. Worth remembering: when a live check disagrees with a unit test, suspect the fixture before the code.

## Not done

- Only **one** theme is used per shelf. Two themes (one per slot) would be reasonable; deliberately kept to one so the shelf does not read as a topic list.
- The theme words come from a stop-word-filtered extractor, so unusual spellings surface as written. That is intentional — it is the writer's word — but it means a typo could appear as a prompt.
